# Personas

## P1 — Dana, the conversational user
Dana is deaf or hard of hearing and is talking face-to-face with a hearing person who speaks another language, for example at a clinic desk, a shop or a family visit. Dana and the other speaker take turns, and each speaks their own language. Dana needs a tool that is fast to start, works on a laptop placed between them, and makes it obvious whose turn it is.
- Success: a back-and-forth conversation without either person handling settings mid-conversation.
- Pain points: setup friction, a translation in the wrong direction after a turn change, and small text.

## P2 — Marco, the presenter / event operator
Marco runs captions for a talk, service or meeting. One person speaks in one language, and the audience reads a different language on a projector, TV or OBS overlay.
- Success: a clean, large, single-language translated feed that runs for an hour without anyone touching it.
- Pain points: lag build-up, original-language text leaking onto the audience screen, and language packs downloading during the live event.

## P3 — Priya, the bilingual / learning viewer
Priya, or her audience, benefits from seeing the original words and the translation together. This could be a language learner, a bilingual audience, or a deaf person who wants to check the translation against the source.
- Success: two aligned panes with the same segment visible in both, arranged to fit the screen shape.
- Pain points: panes drifting out of sync and RTL text rendering incorrectly.
